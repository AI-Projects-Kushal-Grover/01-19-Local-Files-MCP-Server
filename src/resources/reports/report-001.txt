1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------
This report provides a structural and aesthetic evaluation of the 
Metropolitan Library building. Built in 1974, the structure exhibits 
core characteristics of Late Brutalist architecture. While the reinforced 
concrete framework remains sound, significant upgrades are required to 
meet contemporary sustainability standards and modern accessibility laws.

2. STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY & CONDITION (FIRMITAS)
-----------------------------------------------
* Foundation & Frame: Visual inspections and ultrasonic testing indicate 
  the load-bearing concrete pillars show no signs of critical failure.
* Facade Assessment: Minor spalling observed on the northern exposure 
  due to prolonged moisture retention. Remedial sealing is urgent.
* Roofing: The flat roof configuration has reached the end of its 
  functional lifecycle. Minor water ingress noted in the West Wing archive.

3. FUNCTIONAL UTILITY & ACCESSIBILITY (UTILITAS)
-------------------------------------------------
* Spatial Flow: The current layout restricts modern collaborative study. 
  Interior partition walls should be reassigned to create open-plan areas.
* ADA Compliance: The main entrance complies with grade requirements, 
  but the mezzanine level lacks elevator access. 
* HVAC Systems: The existing pneumatic climate control is highly 
  inefficient, contributing to elevated operational costs.

4. AESTHETIC & HISTORIC VALUE (VENUSTAS)
----------------------------------------
* Architectural Merit: The building is a prime local example of geometric 
  modernism. Preservation of the raw concrete texture (béton brut) and 
  monumental waffle-slab ceilings is highly recommended.
* Daylighting: The narrow ribbon windows limit natural light penetration, 
  requiring an optimized artificial lighting design to lift the interior 
  ambiance.

5. RECOMMENDATIONS & NEXT STEPS
-------------------------------
1. Immediate: Implement localized concrete patch repairs and apply a 
   hydrophobic coating to the northern facade.
2. Medium-Term: Replace the current roof membrane with a sustainable 
   "green roof" system to improve thermal insulation.
3. Long-Term: Install a centralized variable refrigerant flow (VRF) 
   HVAC system and integrate a glass elevator column for mezzanine access.
