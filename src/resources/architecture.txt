========================================================================
                      INTRODUCTION TO ARCHITECTURE
========================================================================

Architecture is both the art and the science of designing and constructing
buildings and other physical structures. It is a multi-disciplinary field
that blends creativity, engineering, history, and social science to shape
the environments in which human beings live, work, and play.

Core Pillars of Architecture
----------------------------
First defined by the Roman architect Vitruvius in his treatise 'De architectura',
good architecture must satisfy three basic principles:

1. Durability (Firmitas) - A structure must stand up robustly and remain
   in good condition over time.
2. Utility (Utilitas) - It must be functional and fit for the purpose
   for which it was built.
3. Beauty (Venustas) - It should be aesthetically pleasing and lift the
   human spirit.

Prominent Architectural Styles Through History
----------------------------------------------
* Classical Architecture (Ancient Greece and Rome): Emphasized symmetry,
  columns, and precise geometric proportions.
* Gothic Architecture (12th - 16th Century): Known for pointed arches,
  ribbed vaults, and flying buttresses, allowing for soaring heights and 
  large stained-glass windows.
* Baroque Architecture (16th - 18th Century): Characterized by dramatic
  lighting, rich colors, and opulent, theatrical ornamentation.
* Modernism (20th Century): Focused on the philosophy "form follows function,"
  utilizing industrial materials like reinforced concrete, steel, and glass
  with minimal decoration.
* Contemporary Architecture (21st Century): Lacks a single dominant style; 
  focuses on expressive forms, sustainability, advanced digital engineering,
  and integration with local environments.

The Role of the Modern Architect
--------------------------------
Today, architects do not just sketch buildings. They must consider 
sustainability (green architecture), urban planning, accessibility laws, 
material lifecycles, and structural safety. They bridge the gap between 
a client's imaginative vision and the physical reality of construction.
